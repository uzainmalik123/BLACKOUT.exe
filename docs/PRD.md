> **This file is the source of truth for what gets built.** `docs/GAME_PLAN.md` explains the *why*; this file says *exactly what*. If code and this file disagree, the code is wrong.

## 1. Platform (D-03)

- **A hosted web page plus one small server.** The page is the game (React + Vite). The server runs `kiro-cli acp` with our `KIRO_API_KEY` and nothing else (D-04). The rules require a working URL for judges, so hosting is a must (SYS-18); it runs on **Fly.io**: one small container serves the page and the Kiro server (D-20).
- **Desktop only.** Below 1024 px wide, or on touch-only devices, a "play on a computer" screen shows instead.
- **No accounts, no saves.** Each run is self-contained.
- **The game works without the server.** If Kiro is unreachable, every Kiro feature falls back to rule-written text (KIRO-09). A judge never gets a broken game.
- **One run ≈ 10–12 minutes on Medium.** **1 real second = 30 game seconds** _(tune)_, so 12 minutes covers a 6-hour incident. The in-game clock shows game time. **The shift ends** at Easy ~20, Medium ~16, Hard ~13 real minutes _(tune)_ (D-118): if nothing has ended the run by then, the judge hands down a verdict (clean handover, rough handover, or walked away: a loss).

---

## 2. The core loop

```
Menu → Scenario + difficulty → Briefing (timer) → Live incident → Ending (or the shift ends: a verdict) → Post-incident report → Menu
```

During the live incident, three things happen at once:
1. **Death** (the Director, SYS-02) advances his attack chain by replaying recorded moves (ATK).
2. **Minions** (MIN) keep turning up and have to be closed too, but they're far easier than Death.
3. **The player** investigates and acts through the terminal and consoles (DEF), with **Kiro** (KIRO) helping at its own real pace.

## 4. Minions (MIN, D-09)

Minions are **real background attackers**: the bots every internet-facing server gets. Unlike Death, each minion **does real damage if ignored** and **comes back in waves**, so the player has to keep dealing with them ("it's these guys again"). Each is closed with **one** correct action, which is the difference from Death.

| ID | Minion (look) | Real behaviour | Damage if ignored | Closed by | Waves (Medium) _(tune)_ | Pri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MIN-01 | **Crawlers** (skittering rats) | WordPress/PHP exploit scanners: `/wp-admin`, `/.env`, `/phpmyadmin` | Small heartbeat drain from load; clutters the logs | DEF-12 (scanner user-agent rule) or DEF-06 per IP | every ~2 min | M |
| MIN-02 | **Wraith swarm** (wisps) | Credential stuffing: leaked email/password pairs against `/rest/user/login` from many IPs | Steals 1 soul per success (customers who reused passwords) **and** hands Death a `user-session` (D-18). A wave lands at most **1 success** (2 on Hard) and replays its recording only up to it (D-41) | DEF-07 (login rate limit); blocking IPs one by one doesn't work (many IPs) | every ~3 min | M |
| MIN-03 | **Gnawer** (a fat grub) | A scraper hammering `/api/Products` | Heartbeat drain from slowdown | DEF-12 (rate limit on `/api/*`) | every ~4 min | S |
| MIN-04 | **Poltergeist** (a flicker) | Spam bot posting to `/api/Feedbacks`, getting past the shop's CAPTCHA with the answer `GET /rest/captcha/` hands back (KI-03) | Heartbeat drain (customers see spam) | DEF-10 (feedback CAPTCHA on: a proper challenge that never returns its answer; stops every spam bot whatever its address, never Death; D-133) or DEF-12 (rate limit on `POST /api/Feedbacks`) | every ~5 min | S |

**The feedback CAPTCHA (D-133).** Juice Shop's own CAPTCHA returns its answer with the question, so a bot posts it back. Turning the CAPTCHA on (DEF-10) means deploying a proper challenge whose answer stays on the server: it stops every spam bot (MIN-04), from any address, with no per-address limit, and never stops Death (no move of his posts feedback). Customers solve it as they always did (SYS-19).

A closed minion type stays closed until a variant gets round it (e.g. MIN-01 returns with a new user agent on Hard). **Easy:** Kiro offers to close a minion for you (KIRO-04). **Hard:** more waves.

**Pests, not bosses (D-41)** _(tune)_: a success is one recorded `exfil` (a MIN-02 break-in). Each wave lands at most **1 success on Easy/Medium, 2 on Hard**, and replays its recording only up to and including that success (each wave carries on where the last one stopped), so every line shown is a real recorded line. Over a whole run minions take at most **5 % of the souls**; past that a success still hands Death a `user-session` but takes no soul. **One wave at a time**, of any kind: a wave that falls due while another runs waits for it. On **Medium and Hard** Death may run a move alongside a wave (the "minion + attack" combo); on **Easy** they never overlap.

### 4.1 Leaky taps (TAP, D-42)

Small, obvious exposures a real shop leaves open: not Death's doors, but anyone (his bots and him alike) scrapes them. They open over time and keep dripping customer data until the player closes them with **one** action; easy to close, easy to forget in the rush. Every row is a real Juice Shop v20.4.0 exposure that leaks customers' `Users` rows with no login (KI-03). **Each tap plays its recording** (D-130): a scraper on the rig hitting the real endpoint; the opening is its first request, each drip the next.

| ID | Tap | Real exposure | What the defender sees | Rows it leaks | Closed by | Pri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TAP-01 | **Open logs** | The shop's own access logs are listed and served at `/support/logs` (`serveIndex('logs')`) | An alert; `GET /support/logs/access.log.<date>` `200`s from scraper addresses | The customers whose emails really appear in the logs: the forgot-password page puts the email in the URL (`/rest/user/security-question?email=`), so those who looked up a security question before the scrape (5 in TAP-01.a, D-135) | DEF-12 (block path `GET /support/logs`) | M |
| TAP-02 | **Photo wall** | `GET /rest/memories` returns each memory with its poster's whole `Users` row (no login; password hash and TOTP secret included) | An alert; `GET /rest/memories` `200`s with large responses | The 5 accounts with a memory | DEF-12 (block path `GET /rest/memories`) | M |
| TAP-03 | **Reviews** | `GET /rest/products/:id/reviews` shows each reviewer's email as the author (no login) | An alert; `GET /rest/products/<n>/reviews` `200`s walking product ids | The 12 accounts with a review | DEF-12 (block path `GET /rest/products/*/reviews`) | S |

_(tune)_ A tap opens about every **4–5 real minutes on Easy, 3–4 on Medium, 2–3 on Hard** (seeded; the first one interval in), **at most 2 open at once**, each once per run. An open tap drips **about 1 % of the total souls once per real minute** (3 of 325), first a minute after it opens, from the rows it really leaks, until it is closed; nothing drips while the plug is pulled. Each opening raises an alert. Closing it stops the drip at once and for good. **An open tap blocks the win** (SYS-07, D-44): banishment needs every door closed, taps included. A tap whose rows are all gone runs dry (its alert closes by itself), and one with nothing left to leak never opens. Tap souls don't count toward the minions' 5 %.

## 6. Kiro (KIRO, D-04, D-05)

Real Kiro, through `kiro-cli acp` on our server, **on rails**: our rules decide *what* Kiro may do; Kiro does the talking and drafting. **Kiro is slow the way real assistants are**, and the game doesn't pause for it. Asking Kiro is a real decision under time pressure, not a free answer.
